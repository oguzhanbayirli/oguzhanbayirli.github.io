What it does

Four steps, in the order you actually do the job.

1 · Your people, your shifts

Two plain sheets you fill in yourself: who works for you, and what your shifts are. Define Morning 07:00–15:00 once and type M from then on.

2 · Build the month

One click makes the grid — every day of the month, weekends shaded, a colour for each shift, and a dropdown so nobody mistypes a code.

3 · Check it before you publish it

Every mistake a spreadsheet cannot see: the same person twice, four hours of sleep between shifts, a quiet slide into overtime, eleven days without a break.

4 · Hours and timesheets

Hours per person, overtime calculated weekly rather than monthly, and a printable timesheet with a signature line at the bottom.

The checks

These run against the whole month in one pass. Findings go to their own sheet — your grid is never edited.

  • Overlapping shifts — two codes in the same cell whose hours run into each other, like M/H.
  • Double booked — a shift that starts before the previous one has finished, including one that runs past midnight into the next day's.
  • Not enough rest — less than your minimum gap between clocking off and clocking back on. Overnight shifts are measured correctly, not by calendar day.
  • Over weekly hours — against that person's own limit if they have one, otherwise your default.
  • Too many days in a row — runs of consecutive working days with no day off.
  • Unknown shift code — anything typed into the grid that is not on your Shifts sheet.
  • Not on Staff sheet — somebody scheduled in the grid who has no row on your Staff sheet, so no pay rate or hour limit is applied to them.

It will not eat your schedule

Every rule below exists because a scheduling add-on somewhere broke it.

Old months are kept, not overwritten

Building September does not erase August. The previous grid is renamed to Schedule 2026-08 and left in the spreadsheet.

Your cells stay your cells

After setup, nothing writes to Staff or Shifts. The Schedule grid is rebuilt only when you ask for a new month, and a grid that already has shifts in it is renamed and kept, never erased. Reports go to their own sheets.

Shift colours are yours

Colours are read from your Shifts sheet. As many as you have shifts. No fixed palette to run out of.

Overtime is weekly, not monthly

Fifty hours one week and thirty the next is ten hours of overtime, not zero. Averaging across the month quietly underpays people.

Privacy, in one paragraph

There is no server. ShiftSheet runs inside Google Sheets, on Google's own infrastructure. Your staff names, hours and pay rates never travel to us, because there is nowhere for them to travel to.

The add-on can read and write only the one spreadsheet you open it in — that is what the spreadsheets.currentonly permission means. It cannot see your other files, your Drive, your email or your calendar.

Two further permissions are requested: one to display the add-on's own side panel inside Google Sheets, and one to check a licence key with our payment provider. Google classes those two as sensitive, which is why the add-on goes through Google's OAuth verification review. All three are described in full in the privacy policy.

The only thing that ever leaves your browser is a licence key, sent to our payment provider to confirm it is valid. No analytics, no tracking, no third parties.

Questions

Does it change my spreadsheet?

It creates sheets — Staff, Shifts, Schedule, and the report sheets. After that it writes to the report sheets, and to the Schedule grid only when you ask it to build a month. A grid that already has shifts in it is renamed and kept, never erased.

Can two people work two shifts in one day?

Yes. Put both codes in the cell separated by a slash: M/A. Both appear as separate lines on the timesheet, and the checker will tell you if they overlap.

Does it handle night shifts that run past midnight?

Yes. A shift from 23:00 to 07:00 is measured as eight hours ending the next morning, so the rest-gap check knows that a 07:00 start the following day means zero rest.

Does it sync to Google Calendar?

Not yet. Calendar access is a broader permission than this add-on asks for today. It is the first thing on the list once enough people ask for it.

What happens to my data if I stop using it?

Nothing is taken away. Uninstall the add-on and every sheet it made stays in your spreadsheet, readable as an ordinary spreadsheet, forever.

Is the free version a trial?

No. It does not expire and it is not limited by time. Up to eight people, every check, hour totals — free, indefinitely.
