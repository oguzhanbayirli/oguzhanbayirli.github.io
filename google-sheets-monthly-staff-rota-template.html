A monthly staff rota template for Google Sheets, with seven checks before you publish it

A monthly rota is up to 31 columns of letters, and an A on Tuesday beside an M on Wednesday looks like any other pair. It means 8 hours off between a 23:00 finish and a 07:00 start, and nothing in the grid says so. This page builds the month in Google Sheets with formulas you can read, runs through seven checks worth doing before you publish it, and is straight about what a spreadsheet rota will not do.

Made in Google Sheets, not imported into it

A rota template is four things: a staff list, a shift list, a month of dates with names down the side, and something that checks what you typed.

Plenty of rota templates are Excel workbooks with a line telling you to import them into Google Sheets. This one was built in Google Sheets. Open the free copy, sign in and choose Make a copy, and it goes into your own Drive. You need a Google account and nothing else: no sign-up, no email and no card with us.

It adds a ShiftSheet menu to the menu bar with the rota in four steps, 1. Set up Staff & Shifts, 2. Build this month, 3. Check for problems and 4. Totals, and Timesheet for one person below them. Installed this way it asks for one permission and nothing else: to read and write the spreadsheet it sits in. The whole script is in your copy under Extensions, Apps Script, so you can read what it does.

Or build your own. The rest of this page is that build, one part per section, and each section ends with what the copy does for the same part.

Staff and shifts before the grid

Two short lists, and every formula below reads from them. Same layout as the seven mistakes page, plus the break and rate columns the other two guides add and one cell on the Schedule sheet.

Staff

A2:A25 one name per row, D2:D25 a weekly hour limit for anyone who has their own, E2:E25 an hourly rate if you want costs later.

Shifts

A2:A15 shift code, B start time, C end time, D paid hours (a formula, never typed), E unpaid break in whole minutes. B and C hold real times, not text.

Schedule

A1 any date in the month, B1:AF1 the dates (a formula), A2:A25 names, B2:AF25 the grid you type codes into.

Hours

B2:AF25 the same grid in hours instead of codes, one formula per cell. Nothing on it is typed by hand.

Keep codes short: M 07:00 to 15:00, A 15:00 to 23:00, N 23:00 to 07:00, each with a 30 minute unpaid break. Codes for days off, holiday and sickness can leave the times empty and come out as 0 hours. Then in Shifts!D2: 

=ARRAYFORMULA(IF(A2:A15="","",MOD(C2:C15-B2:B15,1)*24-IF(E2:E15="",0,E2:E15)/60))

That is the formula from the night shift hours page. MOD turns the night's minus 16 into 8 and the break comes off afterwards, so all three shifts come out at 7.5 hours. Every total on this page is built on this column, so read it by eye before going on.

Names must match exactly between Staff and Schedule. Kate on one and Katie on the other are two people, and the one on the grid quietly gets the default limit and a rate of zero. Leave D blank for anyone on the default; the weekly overtime page has the formula that falls back to it.

In the copy, each person can have their own weekly hour limit in the Staff sheet's Max hours / week column, and everyone else gets the default. The Shifts sheet takes an unpaid break in minutes for each code, and unpaid breaks are taken off each shift's hours.

Building the month

One cell decides which month the tab is. The date row, the weekends and the length of the month all follow from it.

Type any date in October 2026 into Schedule!A1, then in B1:

=SEQUENCE(1,DAY(EOMONTH(A1,0)),EOMONTH(A1,-1)+1)

EOMONTH(A1,0) is the last day of the month, so DAY of it is the length, 31 for October and 30 for November, and EOMONTH(A1,-1)+1 is the 1st whatever date you typed. The row spills across on its own and leaves AF1 empty in a 30 day month. Anything typed in C1:AF1 blocks the spill, and B1 shows #REF!.

Format B1:AF1 with Format, Number, Custom number format, ddd d, and October reads Thu 1, Fri 2, Sat 3 while the cells stay real dates. Then View, Freeze, 1 row, and View, Freeze, 1 column.

Type the names, do not pull them in

A FILTER of the Staff sheet in A2:A25 is tempting. Do not. The codes are typed and stay in their rows; names made by a formula do not. Add someone halfway down the Staff list, or sort it, and every name below slides down a row while the shifts stay put. From there down, every shift sits next to the wrong person, and the totals still add up. Type the names, or paste them in as values.

A dropdown, and the one setting that matters

Select B2:AF25, then Data, Data validation, Add rule, Dropdown (from a range), pointed at Shifts!A2:A15. Under Advanced options, Reject the input refuses anything that is not on the list, and that includes M/A. Show a warning lets doubles in and typos with them, marked by a small red corner. If nobody works doubles, reject. If they do, warn, and let the checks below catch the typos.

Weekends and colours

Select B1:AF25, then Format, Conditional formatting, Custom formula is:

=AND(B$1<>"",WEEKDAY(B$1,2)>5)

Light grey fill. WEEKDAY(date,2) numbers Monday as 1, so anything over 5 is the weekend. The first half matters: a blank cell is day zero, which Sheets dates to Saturday 30 December 1899, so without it the empty columns of a short month turn grey.

For colours, one rule per code on B2:AF25: Text is exactly, M, and a fill, then the same for A and N. Keep them above the weekend rule, because where two rules match one cell Sheets goes with the higher one, and a Saturday night shift should look like a night shift. M/A matches none of them and stays plain, which is not a bad way for doubles to stand out.

In the copy, 2. Build this month does this in one click: it draws the month grid with weekends shaded, a colour for each of your shifts and a dropdown of your shift codes in every cell. Two shifts in one cell, written as M/A, are allowed. It does not fill the rota in for you.

Unknown codes

Select B2:AF25, then Format, Conditional formatting, Custom formula is:

=AND(B2<>"",ISERROR(FIND("/",B2)),COUNTIF(INDIRECT("Shifts!A2:A15"),B2)=0)

Red fill, dragged to the top of the list. A code with a trailing space looks fine and matches nothing, and this turns it red as you type. The INDIRECT is there because a conditional format will not take a reference to another sheet written out directly, only one handed over as text. The FIND skips cells with a slash, so doubles are not checked here.

Short rest, as a list of bad pairs

Measuring rest properly takes the date and time of every finish, which the mistakes page builds with four mirror grids. With three working codes there is a shorter way: only nine pairs can follow each other on consecutive days, and you can work them out once.

A finishes at 23:00 and M starts at 07:00, so A then M is 8 hours. N finishes at 07:00, so N then M is 0 hours and N then A is 8 hours. The other six are 16 hours or more, so against an 11 hour minimum three pairs are short. Type them from Schedule!A32 down as A-M, N-M and N-A, select C2:AF25, one column in from the first day, and add: 

=COUNTIF($A$32:$A$40,B2&"-"&C2)>0

Each cell joins yesterday's code to today's and looks the pair up, so Wednesday's M goes orange after Tuesday's A. Put the rule above the shift colours, or the colour wins and the warning never shows. That works. It is also a list you keep by hand: change a shift time and it is wrong until you redo it, and a fourth code turns nine pairs into sixteen. It compares a day only with the day before, cannot see inside a double, and never compares the 1st with the month before.

In the copy, 3. Check for problems runs all seven across the whole month. Findings go to a separate Issues sheet, one line per problem with the person, the date and what is wrong, and the check never writes to your grid. Five of the seven also give the cell. A week over its limit and a run of days belong to no single cell, so those two leave it blank.

The limits are your numbers

Every limit on a rota is a number somebody chose. The copy starts at 11 hours of rest, 45 hours a week and 6 days in a row, and all three can be changed.

In your own sheet, give each limit one labelled cell and point the formulas at it. A 45 typed into a formula and dragged across the grid is 45 in 744 places. The pairs list breaks this rule because it has to, so write the 11 next to it where the next person will see it.

Keep two look-alike numbers apart. A weekly limit is where you want a warning; an overtime threshold is where pay changes. By default the copy flags anyone over 45 hours in a week and counts overtime after 40, and every one of those limits is a number you can change on the Settings sheet.

None of this is legal advice. The copy does not enforce employment law: the limits are the numbers you enter, so check your contracts and the rules where you work first. And a limit written as an average over several weeks is not a cap on every week. A check set to that number sees one week at a time, and it will flag weeks that are allowed.

Night shifts and hours per person

A night shift belongs to the day it starts and runs 8 hours, not minus 16. After that, hours per person is a lookup, until somebody works a double.

The Shifts column already handles the night. An N on Monday 5 October 2026 counts 7.5 hours in Monday's column, although most of it is worked on Tuesday. That is the start-day rule, and the night shift hours page shows how it can decide whether you owe overtime.

On the Hours sheet, put this in B2 and drag it right to AF and down to row 25:

=IF(Schedule!B2="",0,SUMPRODUCT(IFERROR(VLOOKUP(TRIM(SPLIT(Schedule!B2,"/")),Shifts!$A$2:$D$15,4,FALSE),0)))

SPLIT breaks the cell at the slash, TRIM takes off stray spaces and VLOOKUP finds each code's hours, so an M/A on Tuesday 6 October is 7.5 plus 7.5, 15 hours. A single code splits into itself. The row formula on the mistakes page gives 0 for the same cell, because M/A is not a code, and the unknown code rule above skips it, so nothing turns red. A person's month is then =SUM(Hours!B2:AF2), and weekly totals come off this grid the way the weekly overtime page builds them.

One blind spot survives. A typo inside a double, M/Q, counts the M and quietly drops the Q, and the unknown code rule skips cells with a slash. Count the doubles with =COUNTIF(Schedule!B2:AF25,"*/*") and read those cells by eye.

In the copy, a 23:00 to 07:00 shift is read as eight hours ending the next morning, not minus sixteen, and rest is measured from the real finishing time rather than by calendar day. 4. Totals gives normal, overtime and total hours per person, with overtime hours worked out week by week, after 40 hours by default, rather than by averaging the month. When a week runs across two months, the Totals sheet names that week instead of quietly under-counting overtime. It does not work that week out: the rest of that week is on another month's grid, and Totals does not read it.

Two shifts in one cell appear as separate lines on the timesheet. Timesheet for one person lists the date, day, shift code, shift, start, end, break and hours, then days worked, overtime hours and the total, with an employee and a manager signature line. It is built from the rota, so it shows what was planned: if a shift was swapped, change the grid before you print.

Next month without losing this one

The obvious move, renaming this month's tab and starting a fresh one, is the one that quietly breaks every formula that reads the grid.

Renaming a tab rewrites every formula that points at it. Rename Schedule to Schedule 2026-10 and the Hours sheet and every formula that reads the grid follow it into the archive, and November is checked by nothing. Nothing errors. The numbers just stop moving.

Do it the other way round. At the end of October, right-click the Schedule tab, Duplicate, and rename the copy Schedule 2026-10. That is the archive, and nothing points at it. Duplicate Hours too and paste the copy over itself with Paste special, Values only, or it recalculates from November. The boundary week on the weekly overtime page reads last month from exactly that kind of copy. Then type a date in November into A1, select B2:AF25 and press Delete. The date row redraws to 30 days, and the dropdown and colours stay with the tab.

In the copy, building a new month never erases a grid that already has shifts in it; the old grid is renamed, for example to Schedule 2026-08, and kept. Load an example month on the menu loads a month with deliberate mistakes so you can watch the checker find them before you trust it with real names. It refuses to write over a month that already has shifts in it.

The coverage check, if you want one

The missing check is one you can add to your own grid. Put M, A and N in Schedule!A27:A29 and the fewest people each needs in AG27:AG29, the column after the 31st. Then this goes in B27, dragged right to AF and down to row 29: 

=COUNTIF(B$2:B$25,$A27)+COUNTIF(B$2:B$25,$A27&"/*")+COUNTIF(B$2:B$25,"*/"&$A27)

That counts people on the code alone plus doubles that start or end with it. Then conditional format B27:AF29 with: 

=AND(B$1<>"",B27<$AG27)

Red fill. A night with one person when you need two goes red, and the empty columns of a short month stay quiet. It counts people rostered on a code, not who is in the building at 14:00, which is a question about times, and a cell with three codes counts only its first and last.
